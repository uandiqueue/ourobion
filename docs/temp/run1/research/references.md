### Added in RU3 (C3+D5+D12a robust anomaly baseline)

- `[iglewicz-hoaglin-1993]` Iglewicz B, Hoaglin DC. *How to Detect and Handle Outliers*. ASQC Quality
  Press (Basic References in Quality Control: Statistical Techniques, Vol. 16), 1993. — origin of the
  modified z-score `M = 0.6745·(x−median)/MAD` and the `|M| > 3.5` cutoff. (Primary monograph; not
  fetched directly — confirmed as a real citable reference via scirp.org catalog record and multiple
  secondary sources.) Also in ADR-0002 sources.
- `[nist-outliers]` NIST/SEMATECH. *e-Handbook of Statistical Methods* — outlier detection / modified
  z-score. https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/ — recommends the |M|>3.5 modified-z rule.
- `[metricgate-mz]` MetricGate. *Modified Z-Score (Iglewicz–Hoaglin Robust Outlier Score)* &
  *Iglewicz–Hoaglin Modified Z Outliers*. https://metricgate.com/docs/robust-z-score-modified/ ·
  https://metricgate.com/docs/iglewicz-hoaglin-modified-z-outliers/ — states formula, 0.6745→σ
  rescale, |M|>3.5, and 50%-vs-0% breakdown rationale. (Secondary; backs textbook facts.)
- `[statology-mz]` Statology. *Modified Z-Score*. https://www.statology.org/modified-z-score/ —
  formula and |M|>3.5 as the named Iglewicz–Hoaglin convention. (Blog; corroborating.)
- `[sd-calc-mz]` *Modified Z-Score Outlier Detection: Using MAD Instead of Standard Deviation*.
  https://standarddeviationcalculator.app/learn/modified-z-score-outlier-detection — formula, rescale,
  cutoff, robustness. (Blog; corroborating.)
- `[rousseeuw-croux-1993]` Rousseeuw PJ, Croux C. *Alternatives to the Median Absolute Deviation*.
  JASA 88(424):1273–1283, 1993. doi:10.1080/01621459.1993.10476408 — 1.4826 MAD-to-σ consistency
  factor; robust scale estimation. (Cited via secondary corroboration.)
- `[spc-3sigma]` SPC for Excel. *Three Sigma Limits and Control Charts*.
  https://www.spcforexcel.com/knowledge/control-chart-basics/three-sigma-limits-control-charts/ —
  68-95-99.7 rule; single point beyond 3σ under a normal in-control process = 0.27% (2·(1−Φ(3))).
  Anchor for deriving the ~31.7% fire rate of a 1σ deadband.
- `[quer-2020]` Quer G, Gouda P, Galarnyk M, Topol EJ, Steinhubl SR. *Inter- and intraindividual
  variability in daily resting heart rate and its associations with age, sex, sleep, BMI, and time of
  year: Retrospective, longitudinal cohort study of 92,457 adults*. PLOS ONE 15(2):e0227709, 2020.
  doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0227709 (PMC7001906) — intra-individual RHR ~order of magnitude narrower
  than between-person spread; demographics poorly predict individual RHR → personal baseline justified.
- `[stanford-rhrdiff]` Stanford RHR-Diff / wearable anomaly-detection method (PMC9020268).
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9020268/ — standardizes residuals against a 28-day sliding
  average daily curve; source of the 28-day-window convention.
- `[rousselet-wilcox]` Rousselet GA, Wilcox RR. *Reaction times and other skewed distributions:
  problems with the mean and the median*. Meta-Psychology 4:MP.2019.1630, 2020 (bioRxiv 383935).
  https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/383935.full.pdf — mean is a poor central-tendency measure
  under skew; sample median is upward-biased in small samples.
- `[laerd-central]` *Measures of central tendency — mean, median, mode* (Laerd Statistics mirror,
  PMC7221239). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7221239/ — median is the appropriate central
  tendency for skewed or ordinal data. (Secondary.)

### Added in RU4 (C4+D12b+D14a n=1 evaluator gates)

- `[spearman-town]` Psychology.town. *Introduction to rank correlations: Spearman & Kendall's tau*.
  https://psychology.town/statistics/introduction-rank-correlations-spearman-kendall-tau/ — Spearman is
  Pearson-on-ranks; distribution-free/monotonic/outlier-robust. (Blog; backs textbook consensus.)
- `[surveymonkey-corr]` SurveyMonkey. *Pearson vs Spearman correlation*.
  https://www.surveymonkey.com/market-research/resources/pearson-correlation-vs-spearman-correlation/
  — assumptions distinguishing the two. (Secondary; "Pearson disqualified" claim here was refuted.)
- `[bosco-2015]` Bosco FA, Aguinis H, Singh K, Field JG, Pierce CA. *Correlational effect size
  benchmarks*. Journal of Applied Psychology 100(2):431–449, 2015. doi:10.1037/a0038047.
  https://www.hermanaguinis.com/pdf/JAP2015.pdf — N=147,328 correlations; median |r|=.16; Cohen's
  .10/.30/.50 ≈ 33rd/73rd/90th percentiles → r=.30 is top-quartile, not "medium."
- `[gignac-szodorai-2016]` Gignac GE, Szodorai ET. *Effect size guidelines for individual differences
  researchers*. Personality and Individual Differences 102:74–78, 2016. doi:10.1016/j.paid.2016.06.069.
  https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0191886916308194 — 708 meta-analytic
  correlations; 25/50/75th pct = .11/.19/.29; relabel .10/.20/.30 "relatively small/typical/large."
- `[dobriban-2026]` Dobriban E et al. *On the failure of the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure to control
  FDR under two-sided dependence* (title approx.). arXiv:2607.12208, 2026.
  https://arxiv.org/html/2607.12208 — BH can fail FDR control for correlated two-sided Gaussian
  p-values; one-sided PRDS proofs don't transfer to two-sided. **Non-peer-reviewed preprint,
  AI-assisted derivation, numerically tiny violations — treat as caution, not proof.**
- `[pyper-peterman-1998]` Pyper BJ, Peterman RM. *Comparison of methods to account for autocorrelation
  in correlation analyses of fish data*. Canadian Journal of Fisheries and Aquatic Sciences
  55(9):2127–2140, 1998. doi:10.1139/f98-104 (erratum f98-201). https://cdnsciencepub.com/doi/10.1139/f98-104
  — modified-Chelton effective-N; Monte-Carlo Type-I near nominal; short-series N_eff more variable.
  Also in ADR-0002 sources.
- `[afyouni-2019]` Afyouni S, Smith SM, Nichols TE. *Effective degrees of freedom of the Pearson's
  correlation coefficient under autocorrelation*. NeuroImage 199:609–625, 2019.
  doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2019.05.011. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1053811919303945
  — xDF estimator; Bartlett/P&P-family biased by non-zero cross-correlation. Also in ADR-0002 sources.
- `[meinshausen-buhlmann-2010]` Meinshausen N, Bühlmann P. *Stability selection*. JRSS-B 72(4):417–473,
  2010. doi:10.1111/j.1467-9868.2010.00740.x — subsampling + derived E(V) false-discovery bound; the
  construction a fixed-window sign check lacks.
- `[liu-zhou-2023]` Liu S, Zhou X. *(blocked) cross-validation for autocorrelated time series*. British
  Journal of Mathematical and Statistical Psychology, 2023. doi:10.1111/bmsp.12330.
  https://bpspsychub.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/bmsp.12330 — blocked CV preserves temporal
  contiguity and beats AIC/BIC; standard k-fold not recommended for autocorrelated data.

### Added in RU5 (C5 baseline confidence cutoffs)

- `[ross-2014]` Ross KM, Murphy MLM, Adam EK, Chen E, Miller GE. *How stable are diurnal cortisol
  activity indices in healthy individuals? Evidence from three multi-wave studies*.
  Psychoneuroendocrinology, 2014. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25137484/ — generalizability theory:
  mean cortisol reliable at ~3 days, AUC 4–8, slope ~10; day-count is metric-dependent.
- `[kang-2004]` Kang M, Bassett DR, Barreira TV, et al. *How many days are enough? A study of 365 days
  of pedometer monitoring*. Research Quarterly for Exercise and Sport, 2009 (analysis per PMID
  15533542). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15533542/ — step count needs a minimum of 3 days for
  ICC 0.80; any 3 days sufficient; single day not acceptable.
- `[trost-2002]` Trost SG, McIver KL, Pate RR. *Conducting accelerometer-based activity assessments in
  field-based research*. Med Sci Sports Exerc, 2002 (PMID 12165695).
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12165695/ — MVPA 3–4 days for 80% reliability; physical inactivity/
  sedentary time needs ≥7 days (metric-dependent).
- `[ridgers-2016]` Ridgers ND, et al. *Wear-time and days needed for reliable estimates from the
  SenseWear armband in children*. 2016 (PMID 26971298). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26971298/ —
  sleep (time in bed, total sleep) 6–7 nights for ICC 0.7; activity intensities 5 days; overall 7-day
  protocol recommended.
- `[heijden-hrv-2012]` (HRV baseline trends). Eur J Appl Physiol, 2012. doi:10.1007/s00421-012-2354-4 —
  meaningful individual HRV assessment relies on trends across a multi-day window; no specific day-count
  cutoff. (Supporting the "needs several days" premise only.)
- *Note:* RU5's run was budget-truncated at verification; several relevant leads (a 14-day EMA baseline
  study; actigraphy 5-vs-7-night sleep-metric distinctions, PMID 9989370) were **unverified due to the
  session limit, not refuted** — candidates for a future top-up pass, not cited as evidence here.
